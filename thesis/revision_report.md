## Major changes

**The corrected account of NEAT (W-01a/b/c).** Three passages claimed NEAT "adds a node or a connection only when the addition improves fitness." This misstates the algorithm the whole thesis rests on, and it contradicts the speciation rationale the same paragraphs give — speciation exists *because* new structure usually lowers fitness at first. All three now say structure is added by random mutation and retained only where selection keeps it. No new source was needed; the correction is internal to §2.2's own argument and to Stanley & Miikkulainen (2002), already cited.

**Fitness aggregation reconciled (W-02, K-03, T-07).** §3.4 rejected the normalised product for "permitting single-task specialisation"; §5.6 then recommended it as the cure for exactly that problem. The Methodology's version was the wrong one, but the review's proposed fix (the product is min-like) overshoots: a product does drive the aggregate toward zero if a score collapses, yet short of collapse a strong score still multiplicatively compensates for a weak one. The thesis's own calibration data confirms this middle position — the product's mean stronger–weaker gap was 0.18, between the sum's 0.20 and the minimum's 0.11. §3.4 now states this; §6 recommends the product for its actual virtue (it keeps a nonzero selection gradient on the leading task, which the minimum clips to exactly zero) and concedes the imbalance cost, citing the calibration. Both passages now describe the same object. T-07 is folded in: the aggregation choice is presented as structural, with empirical support explicitly deferred rather than silently borrowed from a later chapter.

**Statistical-claim alignment (C-01).** The Introduction promised that the sample and seed counts made an absence of difference "statistically meaningful." §5.2 correctly reports that only effects of Cohen's $d \geq 0.74$ can be ruled out and that smaller ones "could have gone undetected." The Results were right; the Introduction now states the minimum detectable effect and says "three conditions" rather than "the two algorithms." The abstract and conclusion carry the same bound, so the claim is now identical in all four places.

**The abstract (T-02).** Newly written, ~250 words, drawn exclusively from the corrected body: the problem, the shared-genome interface, the three conditions and the design (pop 200 / 750 generations / 30 seeds / 90 runs / 20 re-evaluation episodes), the null with its exact statistics, the detectable-effect bound, the fact that the diversity mechanism *was* exercised (tanh is HA-NEAT's least common hidden activation at 11%), the single structural signal and its attribution to the marker machinery rather than to heterogeneity, and the honest qualifier that the objective degenerated toward single-task Walker2D optimisation. Every number was independently re-verified against §5 by the final auditor.

**The conclusion and the RQ1 answer (T-01, T-06, K-06).** §6 was an empty heading followed by the bibliography. It now runs: a summary of what was done; an explicit RQ1 verdict; an explicit RQ2 verdict; contributions restated at the level the evidence supports; limitations; future work. **RQ1 was never answered anywhere in the original thesis.** The answer given is a qualified no, and it is tied to the reported numbers: the shared interface works (a single genome does run on both bodies with no per-task heads), but the median normalised-minimum fitness is ~0.20, champions sit at a Hopper score of ~0.34 (~1,000 raw reward, the level of a policy earning little beyond the alive bonus), and Walker2D binds the minimum for 83–97% of them. The verdict is therefore "a viable but weak joint controller," not "yes." RQ2's answer is no on the fitness criterion, with the sub-question answered by the ablation: the one structural signal appears in the condition with *no* activation diversity, so it is attributed to the marker-reassignment machinery — flagged explicitly as a hypothesis, not a tested mechanism. The five limitations and three follow-ups were **moved** from §5.6 (not rewritten), with the S-06 number disagreement fixed and a sixth limitation added for the unmeasured modularity claim; §5.6 now points forward to §6 instead of carrying chapter-level material.

**Citation and build repairs (K-04, K-05, plus three bare `\cite`s).** The review's headline `\parentcite` blocker turned out to be stale — the author had already fixed it, and the original compiles. The real citation defects were different. Pascual-Leone (2005) was cited under two keys (`pascual-leone_plastic_2005` in §1, `pascualleone_plastic_2005` in §2.1) that resolve to two *duplicate bib entries* for the same paper — so it printed **twice** in the bibliography. ## Remaining limitations

- **R-09 — retained after validation.** The review flags the 750-generation diminishing-returns justification as restated in §5.1 and §5.2 without new evidence. On inspection both passages are already bare cross-references to §4.5 ("the calibration study … fixed the 750-generation budget"; "the diminishing-returns regime that the budget calibration anticipated (Section 4.5)"), not re-arguments. The argument is made once, in §4.5, exactly as the review asks. No edit was warranted; recorded as a won't-fix rather than left silent.

- **The marker-reassignment mechanism is a hypothesis, not a result.** The conclusion attributes the ablation's compactness to marker reassignment. This is consistent with the code path and the observed ordering but was never tested directly, and it rests on a single significant pairwise contrast ($p = 0.030$) over heavily tied counts. The text says so explicitly in both §5.6 and §6. Resolving it requires the targeted ablation named in the future-work section (activation-mutation rate set to zero, machinery otherwise intact) — a new experiment, not an editorial change.

- **The "hop in place" reading of the Hopper plateau is unverified.** The thesis infers from the ~1,000 raw-reward band that champions may survive without progressing, and states plainly that episodes were not rendered to confirm it. Rendering a sample of champion episodes would settle it and would sharpen the RQ1 verdict; it is cheap and does not require the lost GPU server. Left as-is because inventing the confirmation was not an option.

- **Three duplicate keys remain in `additional_pap.bib`** (`song_highly_2005`, `kirkpatrick_overcoming_2017`, `miconi_differentiable_2018`). Biber skips them with a warning and the bibliography is correct, so this is cosmetic. Not fixed here because the repository's workflow forbids hand-editing `.bib` files; it should be resolved in Zotero.
